## THESIS — AI optical networking / datacenter interconnect supply chain
- **NVIDIA relationship:** Fabrinet is the manufacturing partner for NVIDIA's optical transceiver ecosystem — NVIDIA's own Spectrum-X Photonics announcement (2025) named Fabrinet among industry leaders supporting its pluggable optical transceiver tech alongside Coherent, Eoptolink and Innolight. NVIDIA = **27.6% of FY2025 revenue** (largest customer; primary buyer of transceivers and CPO modules per FY25 10-K disclosures); Cisco 18.2% (top-2 ≈ 46%).
- **800G/1.6T + CPO:** company completed qualification and began shipping **two 800G scale-out transceiver programs directly to a hyperscale customer** — initial ramp in Q4 FY26, volumes rising through FY27; multiple merchant transceiver programs in progress. Industry (Lumentum, SemiAnalysis): 800G→1.6T ramp underway; CPO engines 1.6T–3.2T; NVIDIA Quantum-X/Spectrum-X Photonics and future 1.6T CPO platforms (Feynman-era) expand the optical content Fabrinet manufactures. Networking ≈15–18% of AI cluster cost; transceivers ≈60% of networking cost (SemiAnalysis) — optical content scales with GPU cluster size.
- **Datacenter capex cycle:** hyperscaler AI capex still expanding; Fabrinet is the "picks-and-shovels" contract manufacturer with ~73% of revenue from optical comms, DCI +90% YoY in Q3, demand exceeding supply in Datacom.
- **Geopolitical tailwind:** FCC/Trump administration reportedly drafting a ban on imports of NEW Chinese optical transceiver modules (publish target: end of 2026); Chinese makers (Innolight ~27% global DC-transceiver share, Eoptolink) supply ~2/3 of global units → potential order shift to non-Chinese suppliers (Fabrinet, Coherent, Lumentum, AAOI — the only major US-based manufacturer). Fabrinet's Thailand-based, non-China manufacturing makes it a structural beneficiary; near-term caveat: Western suppliers lack scale to fully replace Chinese volume.
- **Diversification:** HPC $107M (+25% QoQ) and non-optical +52% YoY; new merchant/hyperscale datacom programs de-risk Nvidia concentration over time.
- Balance sheet: ~$940M net cash, zero debt — funds the Thailand capacity expansion (capex $63.8M in Q3 alone).

## RISKS
1. **Customer concentration:** NVIDIA ~27.6% and Cisco ~18.2% of FY25 revenue (top-2 ≈ 46%) — order swings at either move the whole company; disclosed in 10-K.
2. **Supply constraints:** lasers, memory, certain ASICs — already capped Datacom growth in Q3 (−6% QoQ); could persist into Q4 and FY27 ramp.
3. **Thailand concentration / trade exposure:** majority of assets/operations in Thailand; U.S.–China trade dispute and tariff risk on materials (10-K risk factors); FX headwinds pressured gross margin in Q3; new-program ramp inefficiencies.
4. **FCC ban is double-edged:** order-shift upside vs. risk of AI-data-center buildout disruption, qualification timelines, and retaliation; rule could still be shelved.
5. **Valuation/expectations:** ~34x forward EPS (FY27) / ~46x TTM non-GAAP; stock already up ~74% YoY and rallied ~9% into the print — a beat-and-raise is substantially priced in; any guide below street FY27 could disappoint.
6. **FCF swing:** capex-heavy expansion turned TTM FCF negative (yfinance) even though company-reported 9M FCF is positive — watch capex intensity vs. cash generation.
