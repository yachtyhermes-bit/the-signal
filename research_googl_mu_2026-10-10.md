# ALPHABET (GOOGL)

## 1. What it does / role in the AI buildout
Alphabet is the parent of Google: it makes money mostly by selling ads next to Search and YouTube, and increasingly by renting cloud computing and AI services to businesses. In the AI buildout it is one of the few full-stack players — it designs its own AI chips (TPUs), runs one of the world's largest data-center fleets, owns a frontier model family (Gemini), and sells all three layers (chips, cloud, models) to outside customers. Google Cloud revenue grew 82% year over year in Q2 2026, accelerated by enterprise AI infrastructure and AI solutions, and Alphabet began recognizing revenue from selling whole TPU systems to customer data centers for the first time (Alphabet Q2 2026 earnings call, Jul 22, 2026).

## 2. Framing analogies
- **"Alphabet is the vertically integrated utility of the AI age: it owns the power plant (TPUs), the grid (data centers), and the biggest paying customer base (Search + YouTube ads). Most AI companies rent their compute; Google builds its own chips — and now sells the surplus."**
- **"Search ads are the cash register; Cloud and Gemini are the new product lines that cash register is funding — gloriously, and expensively."**

## 3. Why it's cheap/hated — the debate, and where it sits
The market's argument is no longer "can Google do AI?" but "at what cost, and is Gemini good enough?" Two fights dominate:
1. **The capex/FCF trade-off.** Capex nearly quadrupled from $13.1B (Q3 2024) to $44.9B (Q2 2026); Q2 2026 free cash flow turned **negative at –$5.9B** (first negative quarter in the series), and management raised FY2026 capex guidance twice — to **$195–205B** from $180–190B (Yahoo Finance, "Alphabet's Free Cash Flow Just Went Negative"). Consensus for 2027 capex is already ~$257B (FactSet, via CNBC, Jul 22, 2026).
2. **Gemini execution doubts.** A Bloomberg report (early Oct 2026) described internal employee skepticism about Gemini 4; Google abandoned a "Gemini 3.5 Pro" build in June; a shareholder class-action was filed Oct 2 over the Gemini 3.5 Pro launch (Bloomberg Law, Oct 5, 2026).

- **Peers:** Microsoft forward P/E **26.5x** (GuruFocus, Oct 8, 2026); Meta ~**18x** forward earnings (24/7 Wall St, Jul 7, 2026). So Alphabet screens cheaper than Microsoft but dearer than Meta.

## 6. What re-rates it / the kill number
- **Driver:** proof that the AI capex converts into durable, high-margin revenue and eventually positive FCF.
- **Milestone that proves it:** Google Cloud revenue sustaining high-double-digit/≥60% growth with Cloud operating margin holding ≥35%, AND free cash flow swinging back positive — with 2027 capex guidance (due Oct 28) showing a visible ceiling rather than another raise.
- **Kill number:** 2027 capex guided above the ~$257B consensus with Cloud growth decelerating below ~50%, or a second consecutive quarter of **negative free cash flow** while Search growth slips below ~10%.

## 7. Strongest bear case (argued properly)
- **"The capex is a hole, not a moat."** FCF margin collapsed from ~21% to 9.2% in Q1 2026 and went negative in Q2; depreciation ($7.1B/quarter) is only starting to catch up to a $130B+/yr spend, so the P&L hit is still ahead. Management raised capex twice and says FCF "will remain under pressure."
- **"The earnings beat was cosmetic."** Headline +298% net income and $9.11 EPS were ~$6.26/share of *non-cash* equity gains; the cash-based business grew EPS ~23%, and Alphabet tapped every capital market at once ($49.6B equity + $20.3B notes) while pausing buybacks.
- **"Hidden liabilities."** Alphabet is underwriting third-party data-center construction: ~$43.8B of credit derivatives backstopping payment obligations (terms to 15 years), plus ~$7.6B of supplier guarantees (Steady Compounding, Jul 2026).
- **"Gemini is losing the frontier."** Abandoned Gemini 3.5 Pro; internal doubt on Gemini 4; the class action alleges misstatements over the May 19–Jul 16 window.
- **"Antitrust is not over."** Ad-tech breakup was avoided (Judge Brinkema, Sep 2, 2026), but Google still faces >$3.2B in publisher damages claims (which triple if a jury awards them) and ongoing remedies/appeals on the search-monopoly ruling.
- **"Distribution risk."** Meta's fast-growing "Muse" AI agent drove a Google selloff on Sep 23, 2026, on fears chatbots disintermediate Search.

# MICRON TECHNOLOGY (MU)

## 1. What it does / role in the AI buildout
Micron makes memory and storage chips — DRAM (fast working memory) and NAND (storage) — including the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) stacks that sit right next to AI accelerators. In the AI buildout it is the "fuel" layer: every GPU/accelerator sold by Nvidia, AMD and the hyperscalers ships with Micron/SK Hynix/Samsung memory attached, and AI servers need dramatically more of it. Micron's data-center revenue rose fourfold in fiscal 2026, DRAM revenue alone surpassed $100B for the year, and it is working with Nvidia on the industry's first custom HBM4E implementation ("NV-HBM") for next-gen GPUs (Micron FQ4 2026 prepared remarks, Sep 30, 2026).

## 2. Framing analogies
- **"Micron is the lumber yard of the AI boom: Nvidia builds the trophy houses, but nothing stands without the 2x4s and plywood — and every HBM stack is nailed to a GPU."**
- **"If Nvidia is the engine, Micron is the fuel tank. No accelerator runs without memory, and the fuel keeps getting more expensive every quarter."**

## 3. Why it's cheap/hated — the debate, and where it sits
Micron is cheap for a blunt reason: the market prices memory as a **cyclical commodity**, so it refuses to capitalize peak earnings at a normal multiple. Bulls argue AI + long-term contracts have structurally raised the trough; bears say 2027–28 new supply ends the party and margins crash, as they did after 2018 (industry DRAM prices fell ~80% by 2019). The stock ran enormously (+226% YTD) yet is **still ~18% below its 52-week high** — the market is already voting "peak."

**Position (yfinance, close Oct 9, 2026):**
- **–18.0% below its 52-week high**; sits at the **79th percentile** of its 52-week range.
- **~+226% YTD** (from Jan 2, 2026 close).
- **–15.2% off its 2026 high** (2026 peak was June 25, 2026).
- ~5.4% above its 50-day average and **~46.7% above its 200-day average** — a violently extended trend.

**Valuation vs history and peers:**
- Forward P/E **~5.9–6.0x** (GuruFocus 5.95 and StockAnalysis 5.88, both Oct 2026); yfinance prints ~5.0x. (Yahoo Finance's page showed a higher ~7.0x — see CONFIDENCE NOTES.)
- Its own **10-year average P/E ≈ 24.8x** (Macrotrends, Oct 8, 2026) — Micron is trading at roughly a quarter of its long-run earnings multiple.
- **Peers:** SK Hynix forward P/E **3.62x** (GuruFocus, Oct 9, 2026) — Micron trades at a *premium* to its closest memory rival; Nvidia forward P/E **14.5x** (GuruFocus, Oct 9, 2026). The "low multiple" is an industry-wide feature, not a Micron-specific bargain.

## 4. The numbers that prove it's working (FQ4 2026, quarter ended Sep 3, 2026)

- **DRAM $39.8B (73% of revenue), +343% YoY**; **NAND $14.1B (26%), +526% YoY**. Bit shipments up mid-single digits (DRAM) and ~10% (NAND); prices up high-teens (DRAM) and ~30% (NAND) sequentially — the cycle is running on **price**, not just volume.
- **Gross margin 87.0%** (+210 bps QoQ); **operating income $44.6B, operating margin 82.3%**. **Non-GAAP diluted EPS $33.42, +33% QoQ.**
- **Business units:** Cloud Memory (CMBU) $16.3B, GM 83%; Core Data Center (CDBU) $18.0B, GM 90%; Mobile & Client (MCBU) $13.1B, GM 90%; Automotive & Embedded (AEBU) $6.8B, GM 84% — all records.

## 6. What re-rates it / the kill number
- **Driver:** the market re-rating memory from "cyclical commodity" to "contracted, structurally tight supply."
- **Milestone that proves it:** FQ1 2027 revenue meeting/beating $61.5B with gross margin at or above the ~86.25% guide, HBM growing faster than total revenue, and RPO/SCA conversion tracking toward the $150B figure — proving the floor is real, not a one-quarter peak.
- **Kill number:** any sequential decline in DRAM/NAND pricing, FQ1 gross margin below the guided floor, days-of-inventory (already **129, +9 QoQ**) rising sharply, or a major hyperscaler cutting AI capex. A 50%-style ASP drop (as seen post-2018) would gut the 80%+ margins that justify the run.

## 7. Strongest bear case (argued properly)
- **"This is a classic peak, and you're buying the top."** 87% gross margins and 82% operating margins are not a business model, they're a price spike. Memory has always overshot; the 2016–18 capex wave preceded an ~80% price collapse by 2019.
- **"New supply is coming."** Micron is raising FY2027 capex (a majority of the increase is construction), SK Hynix is spending heavily, and Micron's own new capacity (1-gamma DRAM, Tongluo, Idaho ID1 mid-2027) lands just as HBM4 capacity ramps — the classic setup for a 2028 glut.
- **"The low multiple is a warning, not a gift."** A 5.9x forward P/E means the market does not believe $38/quarter EPS persists; SK Hynix trades even cheaper (3.6x). When the cycle turns, trailing P/E looks "cheap" right before earnings evaporate.
- **"Contracts cap the upside."** Most calendar-2027 HBM bit supply is already sold at agreed (if higher) prices, and RPO is measured at *floor/minimum* pricing — great protection, but it also means the stock can't keep re-rating on spot-price headlines forever.
- **"It's a price story, not a demand story."** Volume grew only mid-single to ~10% sequentially; the beat came from prices up high-teens/30%. If pricing rolls over, revenue rolls over.
- **"Positioning risk."** Up ~227% YTD and ~47% above its 200-day average, the stock is crowded; a single miss or a hyperscaler capex guide-down could trigger a violent unwind.
