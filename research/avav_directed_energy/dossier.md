## 0. ONE-LINE THESIS

AeroVironment just took the first-ever U.S. **production** contract for a laser weapon system — a $464.8M Army award for LOCUST X3 under E-HEL — which converts directed energy from a demonstration line item into a ~$500M/year-capacity franchise whose marginal cost per engagement is electricity rather than $4M PAC-3 rounds; the re-rate question is whether SCDE's currently *negative* segment EBITDA inflects as those awards convert in H2 FY2027.

## 2. THE DIRECTED ENERGY CONTRACTS

### 2a. $464.8M U.S. Army E-HEL production contract for LOCUST X3 (announced 2 Sept 2026)
Primary source: **https://www.avinc.com/2026/09/02/avs-locust-selected-for-nearly-500-million-army-counter-uas-contract-for-enduring-high-energy-laser-e-hel-program/** (curl-200, full text verified)

Verified details:
- Awarded by the **U.S. Army Portfolio Acquisition Executive for Fires (PAE Fires)** program office.
- Value **$464.8 million**.
- Company language: *"This award represents the first-ever production contract for high energy laser weapon systems in United States history, signaling a historic transition from prototype to production for laser weapon systems."*
- Structure: **Other Transaction Agreement (OTA)**.
- Deliverable: **"dozens of LOCUST X3 laser weapon systems over the next few years"** in support of multi-year fielding; AV also provides ongoing system support and training.
- Target set: **Group 1–3 UAS**.
- **LOCUST X3 = 30-kilowatt, platform-agnostic**; integration with the Army's **Joint Light Tactical Vehicle (JLTV)**; options for palletized configurations; "analyzing the potential to integrate on an Infantry Squad Vehicle (ISV) in the near future."
- Builds on the **Army Multi-Purpose High Energy Laser (AMP-HEL)** prototypes.
- Production ramp supported by the **$30M Albuquerque, NM investment announced March 2026**.
- **Delivery window:** "over the next few years" (company). CFO Sean Woodward at Jefferies (10 Sept 2026): *"That E-HEL program is going to be over a multiple year window. It's roughly a four-year program."*
- **Production rate:** John Garrity (VP Directed Energy Systems, AV) told reporters AV was previously scaling "tens of systems a year" but is now capable of scaling **"into the 20s per year"**; "initial capabilities" delivered **within the year**. Source: https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/army-awards-first-ever-laser-production-contract-to-av-with-465m-value/

**Nuance the writer must respect:** the "first-ever in U.S. history" framing originates with **AV's own press release**. Wire coverage attributes it accordingly — DefenseScoop: *"what the firm said was the first production contract"* (https://defensescoop.com/2026/09/02/army-awards-aerovironment-nearly-500m-contract-laser-weapons/); Military Times: *"the company stated in a release"* (https://www.militarytimes.com/industry/techwatch/2026/09/03/us-army-launches-laser-production-with-465m-contract-award/); Breaking Defense: *"what the service calls its first-ever production contract for a high-energy laser weapon."* **Do not write it as an independent, uncontested fact.**

**Unit-count arithmetic — reported, not confirmed by AV or the contract:** Laser Wars (a directed-energy Substack) read the Army's FY2027 budget request as funding **two E-HEL systems at $17.4M each with up to 24 more over five and a half years**, from a draft RFP published in January by PAE Fires — i.e. **26 units**, which divides to **$17.88M per unit** against the $464.8M award. Sources: https://defence-blog.com/u-s-army-awards-av-464-8-million-laser-weapon-deal/ ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/laser-weapon-us-military-drone-activity-us-mexico-border/ ; https://theclarity.today/story/the-pentagon-s-laser-weapon-era-is-finally-here-cb60402b. **AV has never disclosed the unit count or price breakdown. Treat the 26-unit/$17.9M figure as a sourced third-party read of budget documents.**

**Recommendation to the Writer:** use *"under $10 per shot — a figure AeroVironment repeats in its own releases, with variants as low as $3 and $5 appearing in its materials and in CBS News' reporting"* — and then immediately add the denominator problem below. The 60 Minutes attribution is **AV's claim**, not something I could independently verify from a CBS transcript; CBS's own September border reporting used **~$3**, not $10.

### The denominator problem — this is the real story
- **Unit cost: $17.4M–$25M per LOCUST system**, per **Laser Wars**, a Substack tracking directed-energy programs, **citing budget documents**. Reported by CBS News: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/laser-weapon-us-military-drone-activity-us-mexico-border/ ; echoed by https://dronexl.co/2026/09/07/army-laser-cuts-cartel-drone-flights-75-percent/ and https://migflug.com/afterburner/amp-hel-army-laser-cartel-drones-border-2026/
- **Implication:** a ~$18M capital unit firing $10 shots needs roughly **1.8 million engagements** to amortize on shot cost alone. Even at a generous 100 drones per system, the *fully loaded* cost per kill is ~$180,000. **This is the single strongest analytical lever in the article — and it is a third-party read of budget documents, not a company disclosure. Attribute it precisely.**
- But: the same source path supports AV's number too. The FY27 budget request's **$17.4M apiece** × 26 units ≈ the $464.8M award — internally consistent.

## 7. THE BEAR CASE (fully sourced — the writer needs a real short argument)

### 7a. The segment that must inflect is currently losing money
- **SCDE: revenue $134.5M, segment adjusted EBITDA −$8.9M** in FQ1 FY27 (10-Q). SCDE revenue **−21% YoY**. ### 7b. Directed energy is still immaterial inside a $2.2B revenue base
- The marquee **E-HEL contract is $464.8M spread over roughly a four-year program** — i.e. on the order of **~$116M/year at most** if perfectly linear, and **$0 in Q1 FY27**. Sources: CFO Sean Woodward, Jefferies (https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/avav/transcripts/740959-jefferies-global-industrials-conference-2026/); AV PR.

### 7c. The unit economics are not what the headline shot price implies
- **$17.4M–$25M per LOCUST unit** (Laser Wars, citing budget documents; via CBS News). An $18M asset firing $3–$10 shots has a fully-loaded cost per engagement many orders of magnitude above the marginal figure AV markets.
