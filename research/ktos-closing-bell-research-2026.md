## 1. EXECUTIVE THESIS

Kratos Defense is the purest public-market bet on the Pentagon's tectonic shift from "exquisite" multi-hundred-million-dollar platforms to affordable, attritable mass — swarms of jet-powered drones designed to be expended in contested airspace. The thesis rests on four pillars: (1) a unique "merchant supplier" model that positions KTOS as the go-to manufacturer for both primes and the DoD itself, (2) sole-source incumbency on the Valkyrie XQ-58A, the frontrunner for the Marine Corps' first operational Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA), (3) a rapidly scaling hypersonics franchise with $400M in FY26 revenue and a path to $700M by 2027, and (4) a structural alignment with a $1.5 trillion national security budget that explicitly earmarks $13.4B for autonomy. If the future of air power is cheap, numerous, and unmanned, KTOS is the factory floor.

## 2. BUSINESS MODEL MOAT — The Merchant Supplier

### What Makes KTOS Different

The traditional defense primes (Lockheed Martin, RTX, Northrop Grumman) operate on a "prime integrator" model: they win massive platform programs (F-35, Patriot, B-21), manage thousands of subcontractors, and generate single-digit organic growth with high margins from sustainment. KTOS does none of this.

Instead, Kratos operates as a **merchant supplier** — it builds the actual hardware: jet-powered drones, solid rocket motors, microwave electronics, turbine engines. It sells these as subsystems and platforms to both the DoD directly and to primes like Northrop, Lockheed, and L3Harris. CEO Eric DeMarco's mantra: "Affordability is a technology."

**Key moat characteristics:**

- **Speed-to-field:** Kratos went from white paper to a flying hypersonic glide vehicle in 24 months for $15M, using digital engineering and AI. The primes couldn't touch that timeline or cost profile.
- **Internal R&D investment:** KTOS makes true internally funded investments to develop products before customers ask for them — Valkyrie was a private venture, not a government-funded program.
- **Cross-supplier neutrality:** Because KTOS isn't a prime, primes trust it as a supplier. It supplies Lockheed, Northrop, L3Harris, and startups alike. It's "Switzerland" in the defense ecosystem.
- **Vertical integration:** KTOS builds its own jet engines (Spartan, GEK), solid rocket motors (Zeus, Oriole), avionics, and ground systems. This gives it cost advantages and supply-chain control that assembler-primes lack.
- **Low-cost design philosophy:** Platforms designed from day one for producibility at scale — not for bleeding-edge specsmanship. Valkyrie costs a fraction of what a equivalent manned fighter would.

### The "Goldilocks" Zone

KTOS occupies a sweet spot: larger and more battle-tested than Silicon Valley disruptors (Anduril, Shield AI), yet more nimble and cost-effective than the Big Five. It's the only publicly traded pure-play on jet-powered tactical drones.

## 3. COMPETITIVE DYNAMICS — Drone Warfare, Valkyrie, Hypersonics

### The Valkyrie Program (XQ-58A)

The Valkyrie is the centerpiece of the U.S. military's Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) vision — unmanned "loyal wingmen" that fly alongside F-35s and other crewed fighters.

**Key milestones:**
- First flight in 2019 under the Air Force's Low-Cost Attritable Strike Demonstrator (LCASD) program
- Flew with an F-22 and F-35 in formation as early as Dec 2020
- **January 2026:** Northrop Grumman-led team (with KTOS supplying the Valkyrie airframe) won the Marine Corps' MUX TACAIR CCA contract — a $231.5M Other Transaction Agreement for a 24-month prototype development
- **April 2026:** Airbus acquired two Valkyrie airframes for the German Air Force's "Jagdbomberdrohne" program, targeting first European flight in 2026 and operational capability by 2029
- **July 2026:** KTOS expanded its Oklahoma City factory by 100,000+ sq ft to ramp Valkyrie, Firejet, and Mighty Hornet IV production. Currently produces ~165 high-performance jet drones/year

**Competitive position:** The Valkyrie is the only flying, production-representative CCA platform in existence. Lockheed and Boeing have concepts on paper; KTOS has hardware in the air. The Marine Corps' selection effectively validates Valkyrie as the baseline architecture for the DoD's CCA roadmap.

**Production targets:** KTOS plans ~40 Valkyries annually by end of 2027. Jet engines (GEK/Spartan) targeting several thousand units by 2027, priced at ~$40-60K each.

### Target Drones — The Recurring Revenue Engine

KTOS has a dominant position in aerial target drones used for missile defense training:
- **BQM-177A:** Full-rate production Lot 7 awarded Feb 2026 — $61.1M for subsonic surface-launched aerial targets with 70 RATO kits
- **BQM-167:** The Air Force's primary aerial target
- **Firejet:** Next-gen jet-powered target system
- These programs provide recurring, predictable revenue and fund internal R&D. North America target drone market is ~$1.83B (2025), growing at ~7.9% CAGR to $2.89B by 2031

### Hypersonics — The Accelerant

KTOS has emerged as a leader in affordable hypersonic systems:
- **MACH-TB contract (Jan 2025):** $1.45B, 5-year program to rapidly expand hypersonic test capabilities. KTOS leads a team including Rocket Lab, L3Harris, Stratolaunch
- **$400M funding from Dept of War (July 2026):** New funding for hypersonic system and other national security programs, began June 2026 and accelerating
- **Dark Fury & Erinyes:** Low-cost hypersonic flyers
- **Zeus & Oriole:** Solid rocket motors powering multiple hypersonic programs
- KTOS is the only company delivering both propulsion AND flyer systems for hypersonics
- Hypersonic revenue expected to double to ~$400M in 2026, reach $700M in 2027

### Other Competitive Wins

- **$156M C-UAS contract (July 2026):** Mobile counter-unmanned aircraft system for Dept of Energy Office of Secure Transportation (single award IDIQ)
- **$446M Space Force tracking contract**
- **$175M Navy radar sustainment project**
- **Airbus partnership:** European CCA pathway solidifies international demand

## 5. STRUCTURAL THESIS — Why Autonomous Systems Win

### The Bifurcation of Air Power

The Pentagon is undergoing its most significant procurement shift since the Cold War. As articulated by the Secretary of Defense and reflected in the FY27 budget proposal ($1.5 trillion):

1. **"Exquisite" platforms remain** — the F-35, B-21,下一代 fighter — but in smaller numbers
2. **"Attritable mass" is the new category** — thousands of cheap, capable drones that can be expended in contested environments
3. **$13.4B specifically allocated to autonomy** in the FY27 budget
4. **Quantity has a quality all its own** — a $3M Valkyrie sent on a one-way mission is strategically more valuable than a $150M fighter that won't risk the mission

### Ukraine as Proof of Concept

The war in Ukraine has validated the attritable mass thesis in real time. Small, cheap drones are reshaping combined arms warfare. The Pentagon's response is the CCA program, the Replicator initiative, and a sprint toward human-machine teaming.

### The CCA Industrial Base

Kratos is uniquely positioned as the only company with a flying, production-ready CCA platform. Competitors are years behind:
- Lockheed has concepts
- Boeing's Airpower Teaming System (Australia) is not yet US-qualified
- Anduril and General Atomics are building, but not yet at Valkyrie's TRL
- The Marine Corps' choice of Valkyrie for MUX TACAIR creates a de facto standard

- Stock down ~37% YTD from ~$77 to ~$48 after peaking near $134 in Jan 2026
- Trading at ~42x forward earnings, ~6x forward sales — premium to defense primes
- The stock prices in near-perfect execution across multiple programs

### FCF & Investment Phase Risk
- Negative FCF in the near term as KTOS scales manufacturing
- Capital intensity is real — factories, test ranges, long-lead materials
- Equity dilution: ~$555.9M in stock issued in 2025; share count up ~12% YoY

### Competition Risk
- Lockheed Martin is pivoting to "expendable" systems
- Anduril Industries (private, $28B valuation) is a direct challenger
- General Atomics and Boeing are developing competing CCA concepts
- If the Air Force CCA program (Increment 1, led by GA) doesn't incorporate Valkyrie, KTOS loses a massive TAM

### Execution Risk
- Scaling from ~165 drones/year to hundreds is non-trivial
- Hypersonic programs are technically complex; a high-profile failure would be damaging
- Fixed-price contracts in a inflationary environment compress margins

### Budget Dependency
- 90%+ revenue from government customers
- Defense budget politics — though bipartisan support for defense remains strong
- A shift in strategic priorities away from autonomous systems would be existential

### Program Dependency
- Valkyrie is the marquee program; if MUX TACAIR stalls or is restructured, the narrative breaks
- Hypersonic franchise is back-loaded — $400M in 2026, targeting $700M in 2027 — requires continued government commitment
