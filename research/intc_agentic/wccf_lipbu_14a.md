As we noted recently,[two of the biggest takeaways](https://wccftech.com/intel-references-sk-hynixs-ex-ceo-when-discussing-its-memory-plans-fueling-the-28-billion-ohio-fab-jv-rumors/)from Intel's earnings call for Q2 2026 related to the 14A node's[risk production accelerating to 2027](https://wccftech.com/intel-14a-enters-high-volume-production-2028-as-risk-production-moved-ahead-to-2027/), and the chipmaker's confidence in hiking its CapEx "significantly" next year, hinting at its visibility into confirmed orders.

Now, according to the Economic Daily, Intel's CEO Lip-Bu Tan has reportedly disclosed that the [risk production on the 14A node will commence in the first quarter of 2027](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5612/9759462). Of course, the 18A-P node is already in risk production.

This accelerated timeline does not come as a surprise, especially as we reported recently that the [14A node reached a defect density of 0.5 in June 2026](https://wccftech.com/intels-defect-density-minimization-for-its-14a-node-is-currently-tracking-3-4-quarters-ahead-of-the-18a-node-trajectory-indicating-an-earlier-launch/), with Intel targeting a D0 of 0.1 to 0.2 by Q1 2027.

For the benefit of those who might not be aware, D0 or defect density measures the average number of microscopic flaws or defects that are detected per unit area on a silicon wafer. As a particular chip fabrication process nears commercialization, its defect density typically collapses, resulting in a corresponding increase in yields.

[![Image 13](https://cdn.wccftech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/coursera-plus.jpg)](https://bit.ly/4cUtBpU)

Intel's David Zinsner also noted in recent days that the 14A process was improving[at a pace that was the fastest since at least the 22nm era of 2012](https://wccftech.com/intels-david-zinsner-says-14a-improving-fastest-since-2012-node-betting-foundrys-future-on-tsmc-fight/).

As a refresher, Intel's[14A is a 1.4nm-class chip fabrication process](https://wccftech.com/intel-gives-rundown-on-14a-18a-and-advanced-packaging-opportunities/)that features:

1.   The RibbonFET 2 transistors and the second-gen Gate-All-Around (GAA) architecture, which allows for tighter electrostatic control over increasingly miniature transistors, resulting in a better drive current and minimum power leakage.
2.   A backside power delivery system called PowerDirect, which moves electrical wiring entirely to the back of the silicon wafer, freeing up the front-side routing for data signals, thereby driving down electrical resistance and boosting density.

Elsewhere, GF Securities' analyst Jeff Pu revealed earlier this week that the yield on Intel's 18A node was currently [hovering at around 80 percent](https://wccftech.com/intel-14a-production-capacity-tipped-to-sit-at-6000-wafers-per-month-by-2027-end-with-18a-yields-at-80-says-analyst/), based on figures for the Panther Lake chip lineup. The analyst also projected that Intel's production on the 14A node could sit at 6,000 wafers per month in 2027 and 24,000 wafers per month in 2028.
