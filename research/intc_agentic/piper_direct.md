Title: Intel’s 14A Process Is Currently Being Evaluated By Amazon, Apple, AMD, Google, Tesla, Microsoft, NVIDIA, And Qualcomm, According To Piper Sandler

Markdown Content:
While Intel's next-gen 14A process currently lacks a definitive anchor customer, it is nonetheless being evaluated by the who's who of the tech world, as the industry desperately searches for a viable alternative to TSMC, as per a new investment note from Piper Sandler.

## Piper Sandler believes the technical data points for Intel's 14A process are trending better than expected

Piper Sandler has adopted a fairly constructive view on Intel's 14A process in a new note, going on to declare that Amazon, Apple, AMD, Google, Tesla, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and Qualcomm are all evaluating the next-gen process amid a confluence of various macro- and micro-level factors.

**Technical data points are trending better than expected**

As we explained recently, the[14A node reached a defect density of 0.5 in June 2026](https://wccftech.com/intels-defect-density-minimization-for-its-14a-node-is-currently-tracking-3-4-quarters-ahead-of-the-18a-node-trajectory-indicating-an-earlier-launch/), with Intel targeting a D0 of 0.1 to 0.2 by Q1 2027.

For the benefit of those who might not be aware, D0 or defect density measures the average number of microscopic flaws or defects that are detected per unit area on a silicon wafer. As a particular chip fabrication process nears commercialization, its defect density typically collapses, resulting in a corresponding increase in yields.

Intel's David Zinsner also noted in recent days that the 14A process was improving[at a pace that was the fastest since at least the 22nm era of 2012](https://wccftech.com/intels-david-zinsner-says-14a-improving-fastest-since-2012-node-betting-foundrys-future-on-tsmc-fight/).

**The industry is looking for alternatives to TSMC****amid political pressure to onshore production**

With TSMC's capacity choked till at least 2028, the semiconductor industry is desperately searching for a viable alternative to cover some of the backlog, with Samsung and Intel the only available vectors. When you add political pressure from the Trump administration to onshore production, Intel emerges as the only viable choice, provided that its 14A process performs in line with expectations.

**Intel's capital-raise last month is a sign of its confidence in locating an anchor customer**

Intel sold new shares in August[to raise around $15 billion in fresh capital](https://wccftech.com/intel-scrambles-to-raise-15-billion-in-new-capital-as-signs-suggests-it-has-finally-locked-in-customers-for-the-14a-node-process/)- later[upsized to $20 billion](https://x.com/i/status/2086993963576336656). While the company was fairly vague as to the exact purpose for which it raised this new capital, it did note at the time that the "offering is intended to further enable Intel to pursue the growth opportunities ahead while maintaining a strong balance sheet and its commitment to an investment-grade rating."

Critically though, Lip-Bu Tan has repeatedly stated that he would[only greenlight significant expansion of Intel's foundry](https://wccftech.com/intels-14a-customer-wins-would-require-a-massive-capex-boost/)after locking in confirmed deals for the bleeding-edge 14A process node. This then provides one of the clearest signs that we have received so far as to the commercial viability of the 14A process, especially as Intel already has around $30 billion in cash, which seemingly precludes the need to raise new capital for miscellaneous purposes.

Also, with the shell of Intel's Fab 62 nearly complete, the company will need around $10 billion to fund the tooling costs to bring online new capacity of ~20,000 wafers per month on the 14A node. As such, Intel's recent fund-raising exercise can easily meet the entire quantum of its upcoming CapEx requirements.

Do note that Intel's[14A is a 1.4nm-class chip fabrication process](https://wccftech.com/intel-gives-rundown-on-14a-18a-and-advanced-packaging-opportunities/)that features:

1.   A backside power delivery system called PowerDirect, which moves electrical wiring entirely to the back of the silicon wafer, freeing up the front-side routing for data signals, thereby driving down electrical resistance and boosting density.
2.   The RibbonFET 2 transistors and the second-gen Gate-All-Around (GAA) architecture, which allows for tighter electrostatic control over increasingly miniature transistors, resulting in a better drive current and minimum power leakage.

As Intel [reaffirmed to us only this week](https://wccftech.com/intel-ceo-lip-bu-tan-reportedly-moves-up-14a-risk-production-to-q1-2027-as-the-nodes-defect-density-races-toward-target/), it plans to commence volume production on the 14A node by Q1 2028, with risk production scheduled to commence in the second quarter of 2027.
