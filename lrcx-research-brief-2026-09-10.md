### Angle: the etch-and-deposition chokepoint of the AI buildout as the chip industry goes vertical

## A. COMPANY IN PLAIN ENGLISH (usable verbatim in an intro)

Lam Research builds the machines that carve and coat silicon wafers into chips. Its specialty is plasma etch (which cuts away material) and deposition (which lays material down — sometimes one atomic layer at a time). Nearly every advanced chip in the world passes through a Lam tool, and Lam is the clear leader in etch, the step that gets harder and more valuable every time a chip gets taller rather than smaller.

---

## B. THE STRUCTURAL THESIS

- **The old roadmap is out of road.** Shrinking transistors laterally is hitting physical limits, so the industry pivoted to going vertical — stacking memory dies (HBM3E/HBM4), 3D NAND past 300 layers, gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistors, backside power delivery, and 2.5D/3D advanced packaging. Lam's own CTO says the 3D era roughly **doubles etch and deposition intensity per wafer**.
- **Every vertical step is etch- and deposition-intensive — exactly what Lam sells.** GAA needs selective etch, atomic layer deposition (ALD), and atomic layer etch (ALE) to release channels. Backside power adds deposition and etch steps. Taller NAND means higher-aspect-ratio (HAR) channel-hole etch. HBM means through-silicon vias (TSVs) drilled ~50+ microns through silicon with sub-50-nanometer profile control.
- **The math is quantified and disclosed.** Lam says the GAA transition adds roughly **$1 billion of served addressable market (SAM) per 100,000 wafer starts per month**, and backside power adds roughly another **$1 billion per 100,000 starts**. That is structural content-per-wafer growth layered on top of unit growth.
- **The demand driver is memory, and memory is sold out.** Micron says its entire 2026 HBM supply is sold out; SK hynix says DRAM, NAND and HBM are booked through 2026; Samsung/SK hynix/Micron are reported sold out into 2027. Sold-out memory means memory makers must add capacity and convert older lines — both are equipment purchases.
- **Advanced packaging is a second growth leg, not a footnote.** Lam guided advanced packaging revenue to grow **more than 50% in calendar 2026**, led by copper plating and TSV etch; at Citi's Sept 9, 2026 conference management characterized the business as growing **more than 70% "this year."**
- **The market frames LRCX as a cyclical WFE name; the argument is it's also the toll collector on AI memory's structural pivot.** You don't need to pick which AI chip wins — HBM, NAND, GAA logic, and chiplet packaging all get more etch- and deposition-intensive, and all of them run through Lam.

**Why it matters to AI:** AI accelerators are starving for memory bandwidth, and bandwidth now comes from stacking dies vertically (HBM) rather than from shrinking them. Vertical memory is physically built with etch and deposition — so the AI memory boom is, mechanically, a Lam Research order book.

## E. CUSTOMERS

- **Who buys the tools:** TSMC, Samsung, SK hynix, Micron and Intel — the leading foundry-logic and memory makers.
- **Where the money is:** June-2026-quarter geographic mix — **Taiwan 27%, China 26%, Korea 20%, Japan 9%, United States 9%, Southeast Asia 5%, Europe 4%** (company PR). Memory was **46% of systems revenue** in the quarter, up from 39% prior.
- **What they're spending on:** HBM4 capacity and 16-high stacks; 3D NAND beyond 200 layers (Lam pegs a **$40B** conversion cycle); DRAM 1c-node transitions (Lam expects its DRAM dielectric-deposition SAM to grow >20% at 1c); GAA logic at the leading edge; and 2.5D/3D advanced packaging (TSV etch + copper electroplating).
- **Stickiness:** Lam's ~$2.5B/quarter customer-support business (spares, upgrades, Reliant, Equipment Intelligence) rides the installed base — recurring revenue that cushions the cyclicality.
- **FLAG (China):** June-quarter China revenue was **26%**, down from 34% in the March quarter and 43% a year earlier. The orchestrator's "China ~19%" is **UNVERIFIED** — possibly a forward-looking mix figure, but the reported number is 26%. Use 26% and "declining."

## F. RISKS / BEAR CASE

1. **China export controls.** China is still ~a quarter of revenue and shrinking under tightening U.S. restrictions; management has flagged a multi-hundred-million-dollar annual headwind from new rules. Further curbs would hit both sales and sentiment.
2. **Cyclicality.** LRCX is still a WFE company. If memory makers digest capacity or AI capex decelerates, orders can stall fast — and the stock has already shown it re-rates violently on macro rates and AI-durability headlines (it fell ~5.7% on Sept 10, 2026 alone).
3. **Customer/geographic concentration.** A handful of memory and foundry customers in Taiwan and Korea drive the majority of revenue; one capex pause at TSMC, Samsung or SK hynix moves the number.
4. **Memory-capex digestion & cleanroom constraints.** Sold-out HBM is a demand signal, but memory is historically boom-bust; today's record memory capex can become tomorrow's oversupply. Management itself cites cleanroom capacity as a near-term limiter.
5. **Valuation & competition.** At a ~32–34x forward P/E after a 179% one-year run, LRCX needs the 3D-intensity thesis to keep compounding — while AMAT's breadth and TEL's dielectric-etch dominance constrain pricing and share.
