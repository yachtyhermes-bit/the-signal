## PART 1 — ZSCALER (ZS)

### 1. What it does
**Plain English:** Zscaler sells subscriptions to a cloud service that sits between an employee's device and the applications they use — instead of routing traffic through a corporate firewall, every connection is checked and allowed individually, so nothing trusts the network by default (this is "Zero Trust").

**Exact role in the AI buildout:** ZS is *not* a compute supplier. It is the **security/access layer for AI adoption** — Zero Trust access for users, workloads, branch sites and now AI agents; data security; and "Agentic SecOps" (automated security operations, boosted by the Aug 2025 Red Canary acquisition). Its AI link is demand-side: agentic AI both creates new attack surfaces and needs its own secure access path. **Critical nuance: ZS is on the *buying* side of the AI buildout.** The memory supercycle that powers MU is a cost headwind for ZS — CFO Kevin Rubin blamed part of the FY27 capex step-up on "the memory crunch, spiking prices, and costs." ZS's own guide and the MU bull case are the same phenomenon viewed from opposite ends.

### 7. Strongest bear case (with the number that proves it)
**The bear case: growth has decelerated to high-teens while the stock is priced at 45–52x earnings, the growth that remains is increasingly bought, and the cash-flow conversion is deteriorating because ZS now has to pre-buy memory at supercycle prices.**

Evidence:
1. **Organic growth is 20%, not 25%.** Strip out Red Canary ($141M ARR) and FY26 ARR grew 20%; FY27 is guided to **16.6–17.4%** — the first time ZS has guided below 20% as a public company. Revenue growth has run ~25% for four straight quarters and the guide steps it down.
2. **The FCF story broke in Q4.** FCF margin collapsed from 24% to **7%** ($60.8M) as capex + capitalized software jumped to $218.5M from $78.7M. FY26 FCF margin fell to 23.1% from 27.2%. On the May call management cut the FY26 FCF margin guide from 26.5–27% to 22.8–23.3% specifically to pre-purchase **memory, storage and processor** capacity ahead of price increases — a direct transfer of value from ZS shareholders to memory makers like MU.
3. **GAAP profitability is still negative and SBC is enormous.** FY26 GAAP net loss $63.2M; SBC **$821.9M = 24.5% of revenue**, i.e. more than the entire $767.1M of non-GAAP operating income.
4. **Sales leadership churn.** Three senior sales departures in 2026, including the CRO in September; the CFO explicitly tied part of the cautious FY27 guide to the leadership change. The new CRO has not yet reported a quarter, and the stock has already been re-rated as if the risk is settled.
5. **Insider selling.** ~$23.4M of insider sales over 12 months with **zero purchases**, twelve of them in the last three months (per The Inference, Oct 23).
6. **Competitive.** Google launched **AI Threat Defense** (Gemini + Wiz + CodeMender + Mandiant) in May 2026; PANW and CRWD are pushing platform consolidation into ZS's SASE/Zero Trust turf. Morningstar calls ZS a "narrow moat" and cut its FV to $250 from $300 in May.
7. **Valuation vs. the target it just hit.** At $255 the stock is above the average analyst target after an 85% rebound — the cushion is gone. The single number that proves the bear right: **FQ4 FCF margin of 7%**, on a quarter where revenue grew 25%.

## PART 2 — MICRON (MU)

### 1. What it does
**Plain English:** Micron makes memory chips — DRAM (the working memory a processor uses) and NAND flash (storage) — and sells them to data centers, PC/phone makers, and car/industrial manufacturers.

**Exact role in the AI buildout:** MU is the **scarcity supplier**. Every AI accelerator needs high-bandwidth memory (HBM) stacked next to it, plus conventional DDR5 RDIMMs, SOCAMM modules, and data-center SSDs for the storage tier. MU is one of only three DRAM makers at scale (with SK Hynix and Samsung) and the only US-based one. In FY26 the AI buildout turned memory from a commodity into an allocation: **DRAM was 73% of FQ4 revenue and prices rose high-teens sequentially; NAND prices rose ~30% sequentially.** Data-center SSD revenue was **~$10B in FQ4, more than 10x the year-ago quarter, and over two-thirds of total NAND revenue.**

### 7. Strongest bear case (with the number that proves it)
**The bear case: this is the widest margin in the history of the semiconductor industry, it is already rolling over sequentially, and the only reason the stock looks cheap is that the market does not believe the earnings are repeatable.**

Evidence:
1. **Margins are at a physical extreme and the guide is already down.** GAAP gross margin hit **86.8%** and operating margin **80.7%** in FQ4 — MU kept 80 cents of every revenue dollar as operating profit. FQ1-27 guidance steps gross margin **down** to 85.95%. As the Motley Fool put it (Oct 23): there is essentially no room left for margins to go higher, and at some point supply is as tight as it gets.
2. **The growth is price, not volume.** FQ4 DRAM bits grew only mid-single digits while prices rose high-teens; NAND bits +10% on prices +~30%. Revenue +379% y/y is a pricing event. Pricing events mean-revert.
3. **Capex is about to eat the FCF.** FY26 net capex $27.37B; FY27 runs at **~$25B in the first half alone** with H2 higher — so FY27 capex likely exceeds $55–60B. Adjusted FCF of $62.31B in FY26 is unlikely to repeat even at higher revenue, and the spend is aimed at capacity arriving **late 2028** — precisely when the bear says the cycle turns.
4. **The supply response is real, just dated.** CXMT's 4F² DRAM announcement (Oct 12) is what triggered the selloff; UBS concedes Samsung could add >150k wafer-starts/month in 2028. Nobody — including MU — will claim 2028 is sold out, only 2027.
5. **"Even at floor prices" is an assertion, not a result.** MU's SCA floors protect the downside only for the >35% of revenue through 2030 that is under SCA, and only three-quarters of that has a defined pricing framework.
6. **The market is voting.** MU is 30% off its high with estimates going *up* — a 4.2x forward multiple is the market saying these are peak earnings, and the memory ETF is down 40% from its high.
7. **Concentration and macro.** Top hyperscalers are the demand; bond yields at multi-year highs, oil ~$105, and public warnings from Ray Dalio, Ken Griffin and Michael Burry about an AI bubble are hitting the most levered AI-hardware names first (beta 2.23). A strike at Taoyuan is a live operational risk.
8. **The single number that proves the bear right:** **86.8% gross margin that is already guided lower** — with capex set to more than double against that peak, the cycle's cash has to be earned twice before it is yours.
