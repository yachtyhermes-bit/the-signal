Constellation runs the largest nuclear fleet in the United States (plus gas, hydro and renewables after buying **Calpine**), and sells that electricity to two kinds of buyer: wholesale power markets (PJM, ISO-NE, ERCOT, MISO, NYISO — it is now the biggest US wholesale power producer) and, increasingly, giant technology companies under 20-year contracts. **Why it matters to the AI buildout:** data centres need round-the-clock, carbon-free power in bulk right where the grid is already tight, and Constellation is the only company with ~55 GW of existing, already-licensed, always-on generation it can sell to them without waiting years to build.

### 5) THE BEAR CASE
Here is the short case, and it is not about demand — it is about *arithmetic and timing*. Constellation's Q2 2026 **operating revenue of $7.50B missed the ~$7.94B consensus** even with a full quarter of Calpine in the numbers, **GAAP EPS fell to $1.42 from $2.67**, **nuclear generation fell to 44,160 GWh from 45,170 GWh**, and the **capacity factor dropped to 93.0% from 94.8%** with 86 planned refuelling outage days. The company's answer is always *"the contracts are signed"* — and they are, but **the largest one, the 20-year Meta deal for 1,121 MW at the Clinton Clean Energy Center, does not commence until June 2027**, nine months after the quarter it is being used to sell; it starts when Illinois's ratepayer-funded zero-emission credit programme expires. The Microsoft contract only starts when **Crane (Three Mile Island Unit 1) restarts in 2027** — a restart that was originally a 2028 event, is a first-of-a-kind regulatory exercise, and has a slip history. The newest agreements don't start until **2029**. So the bull case is: *pay ~21x today's earnings for growth that is contractually scheduled — and therefore contractually allowed to slip — two to three years out.* Meanwhile the market-design backdrop caps the upside: the last two PJM auctions cleared at the **administratively-set price cap** ($333.44 then $325/MW-day), and the backstop auction's $555/MW-day price is available **only to new entrants**, not to Constellation. And in a week when the Fed hiked for the first time in three years with the 10-year at 5%, a long-duration, capital-hungry merchant generator funding acquisitions (the **$715M RISEC purchase**) and a $5B buyback is exactly the profile that gets re-rated *down*. Note the fourth consecutive down day into Sept 14 (−7.1%), the RISEC-deal scrutiny, the analyst target trims, and short interest that, while modest at 2.66% of float, is concentrated enough that "short interest draws scrutiny" made the headlines. **What proves the bears right:** a 2029/30 auction that clears at the cap *again* (proving the cap, not scarcity, sets the price), any Crane restart slippage, a cut to the $11.50–$12.50 guidance, or a third consecutive RTO-wide reliability miss without a successful backstop.
