## THESIS
Vistra is the only U.S. independent power producer that combines all three layers of the AI-electricity stack at scale: the country's second-largest competitive nuclear fleet (~6.5 GW, now contracted out to hyperscalers for 20 years — 2,609 MW to Meta in PJM and up to 1,200 MW to AWS at Comanche Peak in ERCOT, with 433 MW of uprates effectively creating new baseload without new-build risk), a rapidly expanded dispatchable-gas fleet (~8 GW added in 14 months via Lotus and the pending $4.7B Cogentrix deal) that bridges the years-long interconnection-queue and transmission bottleneck, and an integrated retail business plus a preferred-provider stake in the KKR/NVIDIA Helix data-center venture that plugs it directly into hyperscale demand. The market backdrop is doing the rest: PJM capacity prices have set records four years running and cleared at the price cap while the grid falls thousands of megawatts short of its reliability target three years in a row — a structural scarcity that favors exactly the kind of in-market, always-available, PPA-backed megawatts Vistra owns. Yet the shares have been cut roughly a third from their late-2025 highs as 2026 brought a sector-wide AI-power de-rating, GAAP noise from hedging marks, and softer ERCOT pricing — a gap between a strengthened long-term contract backlog and a beaten-down valuation that is the heart of the story: the AI power build-out's backbone has never been more contracted, and the market has never been more skeptical of paying for it.
