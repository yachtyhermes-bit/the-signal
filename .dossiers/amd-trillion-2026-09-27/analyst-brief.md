## (b) THE STORY IN 5 SENTENCES

AMD makes the server processors and AI accelerators that sit inside data centers — the only Western company selling both at scale, and the only credible merchant rival to Nvidia. On Monday September 21 its market value crossed one trillion dollars intraday for the first time, the fourth US chipmaker to do so after Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron, capping a five-session run of roughly a quarter and a year-to-date gain of better than 180%. What actually changed was not a product but a paper trail: multi-gigawatt supply agreements with OpenAI and Meta (each carrying a warrant for 160 million shares), a two-gigawatt deal plus a multi-billion-dollar equity investment in Anthropic, an Azure deployment with Microsoft, and a fifty-thousand-GPU supercluster with Oracle — commitments now measured in gigawatts rather than units. What the market is paying for is a financial ramp that has not happened yet: roughly fifty billion dollars of revenue this year, roughly eighty-eight billion next, and non-GAAP earnings per share forecast to more than double again in 2027, all of which requires the Helios rack platform and the staggered "Venice" server CPU family to ship on schedule and data-center revenue to double in 2027 as management has guided. What breaks it is the distance between that schedule and the physical world — AMD has secured roughly one-eighth of next year's HBM supply against Nvidia's roughly three-eighths, has committed about one-ninth the capital to lock that supply down, does not fully ship its server stack until 2027, and is being asked to fund far-future cash flows into a bond market where the ten-year yield just hit its highest level since 2007.
