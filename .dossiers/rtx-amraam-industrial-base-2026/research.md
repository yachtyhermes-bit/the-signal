## Verified facts

### 1. The AMRAAM award

- **Award date: Sept. 25, 2026; announced Sept. 28, 2026.** The U.S. Department of War release states plainly: "The contract was awarded Sept. 25, 2026." Raytheon/RTX announced it Sept. 28, 2026, and Reuters filed Sept. 28.
  - https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4613107/department-of-war-and-rtx-accelerate-advanced-medium-range-air-to-air-missile-p (DoW release, Sept. 28, 2026)
  - https://www.rtx.com/news/news-center/2026/09/28/rtxs-raytheon-awarded-20-7-billion-multi-year-contract-for-amraam-under-landmar (RTX newsroom / PRNewswire, Sept. 28, 2026)
  - https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/pentagon-awards-rtxs-raytheon-207-billion-contract-amraam-missiles-amid-2026-09-28 (Reuters, Mike Stone, Sept. 28, 2026)

- **Structure: five-year multiyear contract with two option years, valued up to $20.7 billion.** Confirmed in the DoW release, RTX release, and Reuters.
  - DoW: "a contract that consists of a five-year multi-year contract with two option years valued up to $20.7 billion."

- **It is PRIVISIONAL — Congress has not appropriated the full funding.** Reuters calls it a "provisional multiyear contract" and reports: "industry executives have cautioned that Congress has not yet appropriated the funding needed to support the full multiyear commitment, meaning contractors may be unable to invest at scale in components and facilities until lawmakers act." Defense News repeats this, attributing the "provisional" characterization to Reuters.
  - Reuters, Sept. 28, 2026 (URL above)
  - https://www.defensenews.com/pentagon/2026/09/29/pentagon-awards-raytheon-up-to-207-billion-to-nearly-double-amraam-production (Defense News, Sept. 29, 2026)

- **Only ~$240 million was obligated at award**, drawn from Air Force and foreign military sales funds — i.e. the $20.7B is a ceiling, not cash in hand.
  - https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/raytheon-nets-potential-20-7-billion-amraam-deal (Breaking Defense, Sept. 29, 2026)

- **DoW's own capacity framing: the deal is "nearly doubling AMRAAM production compared to previous levels."** This is the department's language, and it is about the production ramp, not a quantity.
  - https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4613107/... (DoW release, Sept. 28, 2026)

- **RTX's production claim: "nearly doubling production in 2025 compared to 2024."** RTX's release says: "RTX has invested heavily in recent years to ramp production of AMRAAM and other critical munitions, nearly doubling production in 2025 compared to 2024." Note the nuance: RTX attributes the near-doubling to AMRAAM *and other critical munitions*, while the DoW framing applies the "nearly doubling" to AMRAAM output levels. (See Unverified/conflicting.)
  - https://www.rtx.com/news/news-center/2026/09/28/rtxs-raytheon-awarded-20-7-billion-multi-year-contract-for-amraam-under-landmar (RTX, Sept. 28, 2026)

- **Stated AMRAAM production goal: at least 1,900 missiles per year**, set in a Feb. 4, 2026 framework agreement with the Department of War — not a new commitment in the September contract.
  - https://www.rtx.com/news/news-center/2026/02/04/rtxs-raytheon-partners-with-department-of-war-on-five-landmark-agreements-to-exp (RTX, Feb. 4, 2026)
  - https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rtxs-raytheon-partners-with-department-of-war-on-five-landmark-agreements-to-expand-critical-munition-production-302678335.html (PRNewswire, Feb. 4, 2026)

- **Neither the Pentagon nor RTX disclosed a quantity or an annual run-rate attached to the $20.7B contract itself.** "Neither the Pentagon nor RTX specified how many missiles the new contract buys or an associated annual production rate."
  - https://www.defensenews.com/pentagon/2026/09/29/pentagon-awards-raytheon-up-to-207-billion-to-nearly-double-amraam-production (Defense News, Sept. 29, 2026)

- **Customer set: U.S. Air Force, U.S. Navy, and 16 international customers** (per the Pentagon contract notice cited by Breaking Defense). RTX separately says AMRAAM is "fielded by 44 countries."
  - Breaking Defense, Sept. 29, 2026 (URL above) — "covers orders for the US Air Force and Navy as well as 16 international customers"
  - RTX, Sept. 28, 2026 (URL above) — "fielded by 44 countries"

- **Prior AMRAAM award for scale:** the previous record was a **$3.5 billion contract issued July 31, 2025** (AMRAAM Production Lots 39 and 40) — a not-to-exceed, firm-fixed-price-incentive, undefinitized contract action.
  - https://www.defensenews.com/pentagon/2026/09/29/pentagon-awards-raytheon-up-to-207-billion-to-nearly-double-amraam-production (Defense News, Sept. 29, 2026)
  - https://theaviationist.com/2025/08/01/amraam-contract-july-2025 (The Aviationist, Aug. 1, 2025, quoting the original DoD award text)

- **Program scale / longevity:** "Now in its fifth generation, AMRAAM operates on 14 platforms and is fielded by 44 countries, achieving more than 7,000 successful live fires in test and combat." It is also "the primary interceptor for both air-to-air missions and the NASAMS air defense system."
  - https://www.rtx.com/news/news-center/2026/09/28/... (RTX, Sept. 28, 2026)
  - https://www.airforce-technology.com/news/raytheon-dow-amraam-contract (Airforce Technology, Sept. 29, 2026)

- **Program economics governed by the $20.7B:** the February framework covers five munitions — Tomahawk (Land Attack + Maritime Strike), AMRAAM, SM-3 Block IB, SM-3 Block IIA, and SM-6 — under "up-to-seven-year" agreements. RTX said the associated investments were already "contemplated in RTX's recently announced financial outlook for 2026," and used a "collaborative funding approach designed to preserve upfront free cash flow."

### 5. Competitive dynamics & the cost-per-interception problem

- **Prime competitive set:** RTX (Raytheon: AMRAAM, Patriot/GEM-T, SM-6, SM-3, Tomahawk, Coyote, KuRFS), Lockheed Martin (PAC-3 MSE, THAAD via L3Harris propulsion), Northrop Grumman (solid rocket motors, propulsion), L3Harris (Aerojet SRM + new missile solutions unit), General Dynamics (munitions/artillery). RTX is the largest of the group by market cap (~$249.6B vs LMT ~$116.7B, GD ~$90.0B, NOC ~$68.5B, LHX ~$44.4B).
  - Market caps from `data/financials.json` (RTX) and yfinance (peers), Oct. 1, 2026.

- **New entrants / low-cost munitions:** The Pentagon launched the **Low Cost Containerization Munitions Program (LCCMP)** on May 13, 2026 — framework agreements with **Anduril, CoAspire, Leidos, and Zone 5** to procure **over 10,000 low-cost cruise missiles over three years beginning in 2027**. Anduril's portion covers a minimum of **1,000 Barracuda-500M rounds per year for three years**, first deliveries H1 2027.
  - https://breakingdefense.com/2026/05/pentagon-launches-new-framework-agreements-to-acquire-10000-low-cost-cruise-missiles (Breaking Defense, May 13, 2026)

- **The cost-per-interception problem, sourced:** The U.S. Army published a sources-sought notice (Aug. 4, 2026) for the **Next Generation Counter-sUAS Missile (NGCM)**, seeking a munition costing **less than $150,000 per round** in a buy of at least 5,000 missiles. DefenseScoop frames it directly: "Affordability of munitions is a growing concern as the Pentagon seeks cheaper alternatives to firing million-dollar interceptors at inexpensive enemy drones."
  - https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/04/army-new-missile-shoot-down-drones (DefenseScoop, Aug. 4, 2026)
  - https://thedefensepost.com/2026/08/06/us-low-cost-counter-drone-missile (The Defense Post, Aug. 6, 2026)

- **What RTX is doing about it — Coyote + KuRFS:** The Raytheon **Coyote** is "a small, expendable, unmanned aircraft system and counter drone interceptor… with the capability of operating in **autonomous swarms**," paired with the **Ku-band Radio Frequency Sensor (KuRFS)** radar as the detect-and-defeat pair in the Army's LIDS counter-UAS solution. In July 2026 Raytheon said Coyote "has been significantly successful combating Class 1 through 3 drones" and the Army disclosed **600 successful combat intercepts** by Coyote during Operation Epic Fury — the system's first confirmed combat use. RTX markets Coyote as a "**low-cost**, rail-launched missile variant."
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon_Coyote (citing The Defense News, July 2026)
  - https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/coyote (RTX product page)
  - https://defensescoop.com/2024/02/09/army-600-coyote-counter-drone-rtx (DefenseScoop — KuRFS/Coyote background; cites a Dec. 2023 Army notice-of-intent estimating a FY2025–29 requirement of a minimum 6,000 Coyote kinetic interceptors, 700 non-kinetic, 252 fixed-site launchers, 118 fixed KuRFS)

(Use with caution — third-party aggregator.)
  - https://exoswan.com/military-drone-stocks (Exoswan analysis, 2026)

## Unverified / conflicting

- **"Nearly doubled AMRAAM production in 2025 vs the prior year" — the subject of the near-doubling is ambiguous.** RTX's own release says it nearly doubled production of "**AMRAAM and other critical munitions**" in 2025 vs 2024; the DoW release says the new *contract* will nearly double AMRAAM production "compared to previous levels." Defense News renders it as "RTX said it nearly doubled production of AMRAAM and other munitions in 2025 compared with 2024." Only Reuters' framing ("RTX said it nearly doubled AMRAAM production in 2025 compared with 2024," per secondary aggregators) pins the doubling specifically to AMRAAM. **Recommendation: say RTX "nearly doubled production of AMRAAM and other munitions in 2025," not "AMRAAM output doubled."**
- **No absolute AMRAAM production figures exist publicly.** RTX has not disclosed a current annual AMRAAM run-rate; the only anchor is the ≥1,900/yr *target* (Feb. 2026 framework). Do not present 1,900 as achieved.
- **The $20.7B contract buys an undisclosed quantity at an undisclosed annual rate.** Both Defense News and Reuters confirm no quantity/rate was specified.
- **TTM free cash flow discrepancy:** summed quarterly FCF = **$10.99B**; the `freeCashflow` field in `data/financials.json` = **$9.88B**. yfinance's stat fields can lag or mix periods. Use the summed figure and note the vendor field.
- **Q3 2026 earnings date is not confirmed:** yfinance says Oct. 20, 2026; Tickeron says Oct. 27, 2026. Neither is an RTX IR confirmation. Present as "expected late October."
- **Anduril's $61B valuation / $20B Army contract:** sourced only to a third-party aggregator (Exoswan), not Anduril's newsroom or a named wire. Corroborate before printing.
- **The "RTX has lagged peers" premise is NOT supported.** See the Bear case below — RTX is mid-pack, and NOC/LHX are far worse off their highs.
- **Brief said "Tomahawk multiyear (~$22B)"** — the actual figure is **$22.9B** (7-year). Use $22.9B.

## Bear case

The strongest short-seller read on RTX and this award, every leg sourced:

1. **The $20.7B is a ceiling, not a contract.** Reuters: the deal is "**provisional**," and "**Congress has not yet appropriated the funding needed to support the full multiyear commitment**, meaning contractors may be unable to invest at scale in components and facilities until lawmakers act." Only ~**$240M was obligated at award** (Breaking Defense). If appropriators slow-walk it, the headline number is decades of optionality, not a year of revenue — and RTX can't fund the factories on it.

2. **Multiyear structure is explicitly framed as a subsidy for capex RTX might not otherwise justify** — the Pentagon wants "stability required for the vendor to expand its workforce, increase production and strengthen its supply chains," and the structure is "designed to entice contractors to move faster." That means capacity investment *precedes* revenue, pressuring near-term free cash flow and margins if the ramp slips.

3. **The binding constraint is physical, and it's outside RTX's control.** Solid rocket motors (Northrop scaling 13,000 → 25,000/yr only *by 2029*; L3Harris building >30 facilities with a missile-solutions IPO still pending), seekers, energetics, machine tools and skilled labor are all multi-year build-outs. RTX acknowledges "hundreds of small and mid-sized suppliers" and is only now "exploring international co-production." Management's own Q2 language — "over two years of quarter-over-quarter material receipts going up" — is an admission the supplier base, not demand, sets the pace.

4. **Pratt & Whitney GTF powder-metal remediation remains a live cash/earnings drag.** RTX booked a ~**$3B Q3 2023 charge** with an estimated **$6–7B gross impact** and **600–700 engine removals between 2023 and 2026** (RTX 8-K, Sept. 11, 2023). Peak groundings were cited around **650** aircraft (P&W estimate; independent trackers higher); shop-visit turn times stretched to **300–360 days vs a 60-day target**; Aviation Week/Leeham expected the disruption to extend into 2027. RTX says AOGs were down 25% YTD and MRO output up 40% YoY as of the July 23, 2026 call — i.e. improving, but from a deep hole, and dependent on MRO throughput.
  - https://investors.rtx.com/static-files/359baa3b-0f38-4949-8b62-d6cf02ebed0c (RTX GTF fleet update 8-K, Sept. 11, 2023)
  - https://www.stratrix.com/crisis-response/rtx-s-pratt-and-whitney-engine (Stratrix timeline, 2026)

5. **Fixed-price / undefinitized contract risk is structural in defense.** The prior AMRAAM award was a firm-fixed-price-incentive, *undefinitized* contract action; the Lockheed PAC-3 award is likewise an "undefinitized contract action (UCA)." Undefinitized UCAs defer pricing risk to both parties and have historically produced charges when costs land above negotiated ceilings. Raytheon's own segment margin (12.6% ROS) is the thinnest of RTX's three segments despite 18% growth.

6. **Legal/regulatory overhang.** RTX paid **more than $124M** to settle SEC FCPA charges tied to Qatari military contracts (Oct. 2024); it has received **SEC subpoenas** related to the GTF engine issue (Feb. 2024); and its 10-K/10-Q disclose a **DOJ DPA-2 and False Claims Act settlement** plus legacy Raytheon Missiles & Defense defective-pricing claims. Also disclosed: U.S. government contracts carry "a broad right to unilaterally terminate contracts for convenience."
  - https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-171 (SEC, Oct. 2024)
  - https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/rtx-receives-subpoenas-sec-over-engine-issue-2024-02-05 (Reuters, Feb. 5, 2024)
  - https://investors.rtx.com/static-files/c5021bc6-51d4-458c-8372-bdce22f43990 (RTX 10-Q, April 21, 2026)

7. **Valuation is a premium to the peer set and to RTX's own history.** Forward P/E **~23.5x** vs Lockheed **~15.4x**, Northrop **~15.8x**, GD **~17.9x**, L3Harris **~17.6x** — a ~33–50% premium. Trailing P/E ~32.6x. Peg ratio ~2.17. Net debt is large: **$38.9B total debt vs $8.3B cash**. A miss on the munitions ramp or a funding delay would compress a multiple that already assumes flawless execution.

8. **Do NOT repeat the "RTX lagged peers" claim — the data refutes it.** RTX trades **~18.5% below its 52-week high** ($226.88) but that is mid-pack: NOC is **~37.7%** below its high, L3Harris **~37.1%**, Lockheed **~27.0%**, and only GD (**~16.8%**) is closer to its high than RTX. RTX's trailing-1-year return is **+12.4%** (financials.json), and it has beaten consensus for multiple straight quarters. The honest bear case is *relative valuation*, not *relative price weakness*.
