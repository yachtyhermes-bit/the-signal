## 5. THE INVESTMENT ANGLE

### 5a. Why it matters to the AI sector

The AI trade is broadening from the data center out to the **edge** — robots, sensors, and on-device inference ("Physical AI"). onsemi is a **pick-and-shovel power/sensing supplier**: you don't need to pick the winning robot maker if every robot needs power conversion, sensing and control silicon. onsemi already sells into AI data center power (EliteSiC, MOSFETs, GaNEXUS; NVIDIA MGX; Great Wall) and says that business will **more than double in 2026**. Buying Synaptics bolts on the **"connected compute"** pillar — Astra edge-AI SoCs, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth connectivity, touch/display/biometrics — so onsemi can sell an integrated Power + Sense + Connected Compute + Control stack instead of a component list. onsemi frames a **+$30B TAM expansion to $243B by 2030**.

### 5b. Why it matters to the stock (mandatory)

- **The structure is the story.** The original $7B deal was **all-stock** — and the market **hated** it: ON crashed from $118.74 (Jun 25 close) to **$90.65 on Jun 26, −23.7% on 44.2M shares** (~4–5× normal volume) the day after the announcement. Switching to **all-cash removes the dilution**, and the stock is now **up ~5.6%** on the revision. This is the cleanest evidence that the dilution was the objection, and that the fix is being rewarded.
- **No-dilution cash structure protects the buyback.** onsemi repurchased **$332M** in Q2 alone and returned **~105% of FCF** YTD. Issuing ~$7B of stock would have made that math much harder; cash + debt leaves the (now smaller, 389.3M-share) equity base intact and EPS accretive "immediately upon closing."
- **The bear hook — the debt meets a 24-year-high bond market.** Funding the up-to-**$2.45B** term loan lands in the teeth of a bond rout: the **US 10-year Treasury yield ~5.24–5.34%, its highest since 2002 (a 24-year high)**; the 30-year hit its highest since 2004. Debt-funded M&A is more expensive and riskier exactly here — that is the natural bear frame.
- **Is the market pricing it right?** ON trades at a **forward P/E of ~17.7** on the Oct-1 close — well below the semiconductor group (SOXX trailing P/E ~43.8, for context, though not an apples-to-apples comparison) — with a 52-week range of **$44.56–$134.92**, i.e. **~40% below its high** on the Oct-1 close and still ~37% below after today's pop. The market is treating ON as a **cheap, beaten-down cyclical** that is executing a large, debt-funded strategic pivot — the re-rating question is whether cash-funded, immediately-accretive M&A plus a doubling AI-data-center business can close that multiple gap.

### 5c. Bear case (written like a short-seller)

1. **Price paid vs. what it buys.** ~$5.7B EV (≈$4.8B equity) for a company guiding to just **>$385M of Core IoT revenue** in FY2026. The growth asset is real but small; strip out the mature, cyclical HMI/touch/display business and you are paying a rich multiple for a sub-$0.5B revenue line.
2. **A rival bid, and the price went *down*.** Synaptics got an **unsolicited competing proposal** — yet onsemi paid **less** (≈$5.7B vs ≈$7B). Either the "competition" was soft/never credible, or Synaptics' board accepted a worse headline outcome to get "certainty." A short argues the auction found no premium bidder — bearish on the asset's true value. **The rival's identity is undisclosed ("Party A").**
3. **Paying cash at the bottom of your own multiple.** ON's stock is ~40% off its 52-week high. Using cash instead of stock is presented as pro-shareholder — but it also means management either believes ON is too cheap to issue, or that holders of ON stock wouldn't accept yet more paper. Either reading is a yellow flag about the currency.
4. **End-market softness.** **AMG (automotive) revenue fell 2% YoY** in Q2 2026 — the auto/industrial cycle is not roaring. Layering a big integration onto a soft core amplifies risk.
5. **Debt against high yields.** Up to **$2.45B** of senior secured term loan (Morgan Stanley commitment) priced into a **5%+ 10-year** world. Deleveraging depends on delivering the synergies and the AI-data-center doubling; if either slips, the interest burden lands on a stock already trading at a depressed multiple.
6. **Deal won't close until mid-2027.** Nearly a year of integration distraction, regulatory review outside the US (FTC already cleared), and a shareholder vote to get through — with the upside only bookable on close and "after the initial 18 months post-close" for the incremental synergies.
7. **Execution/integration risk** on the largest acquisition in onsemi's history, converting equity awards via a floating Conversion Ratio, in markets that are not obviously in an upcycle.

## 6. UNRESOLVED / CAUTION FOR THE WRITER

1. **The exact bridge to "$5.7B" is not clean — ATTRIBUTE, DON'T DERIVE.** onsemi's IR deck calls the $5.7B **enterprise value**, and its press release calls it "aggregate value."
