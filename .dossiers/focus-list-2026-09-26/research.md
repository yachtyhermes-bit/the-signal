This is the awkward part of the Meta case: **it is the one focus name that is neither cheap nor hated — it just had a very good month.**

### 1.6 THE STRONGEST BEAR CASE (as a short-seller would argue it)

On top of the arithmetic, Meta's own forward-looking language flags that the **youth-related US trials "may ultimately result in a material loss."** Second leg: the growth is entirely advertising. **Family of Apps is ~99% of revenue and its operating income *fell* 6% y/y**; Reality Labs lost $4.62bn in the quarter with no sign of a business model. Third leg: the stock. After a **~32% month**, at the top of its range, with the kill number live, the marginal buyer at these levels is paying for Muse fee revenue that does not yet exist as a reported line.
**What would prove the bears right:** FY26 capex landing at the **$145bn** top with TTM FCF staying at or below ~$41bn; another quarter of **operating margin below 31%**; a second consecutive **EPS miss**; or a Q3 revenue print at or below **$61bn**. Short-seller shorthand: *the ad business is funding a capital programme it cannot outgrow.*

### 1.7 CANDIDATE CALL: **WAIT FOR A BETTER ENTRY**
The operating story is intact — 28% revenue growth with a 3.6bn-user distribution engine is not broken, and 21x forward is not a bubble multiple. But **you would be buying after a ~32% month, at the top of the 52-week range, three days before the highest-stakes print of the year, where the kill number (capex at $145bn) is genuinely live.** 
**What flips it to Accumulate:** any pullback that puts the stock back near its 50-day, **or** a Q3 print in which capex lands at the low end of the range and quarterly free cash flow visibly re-accelerates off $784m.

## 2) ADVANCED MICRO DEVICES (AMD)

### 2.1 WHAT IT DOES / WHO PAYS
AMD **designs the chips that go into AI servers that aren't made by Nvidia** — EPYC server CPUs and Instinct GPUs, sold as full "Helios" rack-scale systems — plus the CPUs in PCs and the semi-custom silicon inside game consoles. **Hyperscalers, frontier AI labs and server/PC makers pay it** (Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, Oracle, and the console makers).

### 2.2 THE FRAMING ANALOGY
**AMD is the second supplier every buyer of AI compute desperately wants on the bid list — it gets paid when the largest customers in the world conclude they cannot afford to buy 100% of their AI silicon from one company.**

### 2.3 THE CATALYST
- **Next earnings: Q3 2026 — expected Tuesday 3 Nov 2026. NOT CONFIRMED by the company.** yfinance schedules 3 Nov 2026 (**a Tuesday**), which fits AMD's pattern exactly (Q3 2025 was Tue 4 Nov 2025; Q2 2026 was Tue 4 Aug 2026). AMD is new to the Focus List precisely because the *last* print (Tue 4 Aug 2026) is behind it and the next one is the near-term event.
- **The number that would prove the thesis:** **Q3 revenue at or above the guided ~$13.0bn ± $300m (mid-point +41% y/y, +13% q/q) with Data Center revenue growing at or above the Q2 rate of +107% y/y, and non-GAAP gross margin held at ~56%.** Management explicitly guided to *acceleration*: CFO Jean Hu, on the Q2 release — *"We expect Data Center sales to accelerate in the second half of 2026."*
- **The number that would kill it:** **Data Center growth decelerating sequentially, or non-GAAP gross margin below 56%** as the mix shifts from chip sales to full Helios racks — rack-scale systems carry more pass-through content and structurally lower margin than selling GPUs alone.
- **Next 6–10 weeks:** the Q3 print (3 Nov); the **Anthropic MI450/Helios ramp** (first gigawatt begins **H1 2027**, per the 22 Jul 2026 company release); the **Microsoft Azure Helios deployment**; and any read-across from the Oracle force-majeure story about whether booked AI capacity actually gets built on schedule — which is the same question that governs AMD's order book.

### 2.6 THE STRONGEST BEAR CASE
**AMD has sold $60bn of future revenue for stock, and it is buying a customer.** The Meta agreement — 6 GW, roughly $60bn, announced 24 Feb 2026 — is structured **partly in AMD shares, via a warrant on up to ~160m shares, or roughly 10% of the company**; a $1trn market cap through that warrant (the mechanic Meta's deal depends on) is precisely what triggered in September. AMD also committed **up to $5bn of its own cash into Anthropic** — a private, cash-consuming customer — alongside the 2 GW deal. A short-seller calls that **paying customers in equity and vendor-financing the order book**, and it is the same pattern the market has already punished elsewhere in AI hardware. Second leg, visible today: **Gaming revenue fell 31% y/y** — a reminder that AMD is not a pure AI story, and that its non-AI segments can shrink while the AI story is being celebrated. Third leg, the margin mix: **Helios is a full rack, not a chip**, and full racks carry networking, cooling and integration content that dilutes gross margin, so **revenue growth can run ahead of profit growth**, which makes the 40x forward multiple riskier than the +41% revenue guide suggests. Fourth: **AMD returned roughly +296% in a year and is at an all-time high — there is no valuation cushion left for a slip.** Fifth, and most timely: **if Oracle's force-majeure notice on "Project Jupiter" is a signal that gigawatt AI data-centre commitments can be delayed by power rather than by demand, AMD has a book of gigawatt-shaped promises too.** An order is not a delivery.
**What would prove the bears right:** Data Center revenue failing to accelerate in Q3; **non-GAAP gross margin below 56%**; the customer-warrant/equity-compensation line growing as a share of the deal mix; or Gaming continuing to shrink while Data Center decelerates. Short-seller shorthand: *gigawatts on paper, equity on the balance sheet.*

### 2.7 CANDIDATE CALL: **ACCUMULATE**
AMD has the **strongest verified fundamental momentum of the four names** — Data Center revenue doubled, gross margin up 14 points GAAP / 13 points non-GAAP, roughly $8.8bn of net cash and a **Q3 guide of +41% y/y that management describes as accelerating** — and the catalyst is a *growth* catalyst, not a hope. The trade-off to state plainly: at a **record high, ~40x forward, +296% in a year**, there is no margin of safety and nothing to catch you.
**What flips it to "wait":** a Data Center print that decelerates, gross margin below 56%, or evidence that the gap between booked gigawatts and shipped silicon is widening (i.e. the Oracle-force-majeure pattern showing up in AMD's own order commentary).

### 3.6 THE STRONGEST BEAR CASE
**The growth guide is the confession.** Zscaler grew FY2026 revenue **25%** and guided FY2027 to **16.6–17.5%** — a nine-point deceleration — and guided **ARR to 16.6–17.4%**, below the 20% organic ARR growth it just delivered ex-Red Canary. Meanwhile **net new ARR ex-Red Canary grew 17%, below the 20% organic ARR growth rate**, which is the leading-indicator version of the same slowdown. Second leg, and the harder number: **free cash flow collapsed to 7% of revenue in Q4, from 24% a year earlier**, because **capex plus capitalised internal-use software jumped to $218.5m from $78.7m** — a nearly 2.8x increase in a single year. A software company that begins spending like a data-centre company while its growth rates converge on the mid-teens is not the same company that traded at 100x earnings. FY26 FCF margin already fell to 23% from 27%. Third leg: **the go-to-market keeps changing.** Zscaler had already been through a sales-organisation shake-up around its fiscal Q3 print in late May 2026 (IBD *"Zscaler Plunges On Weak Guidance Amid Sales Shake-Up"*, Benzinga *"…Crashes After Soft Q4 Guidance, Leadership Shakeup"*, both 27 May 2026 — **headline-sourced**).
